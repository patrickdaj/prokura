Open-source reference architecture

Agentic identity, assembled

An AI agent that acts for you needs your authority — and today the only way to grant it is to hand over your passwords and provider tokens. Every agent becomes an unaccountable copy of you. Prokura makes that authority delegated, scoped, human-gated, and revocable instead — so an agent acts on your behalf without ever holding your secrets, and you can always answer who authorized it, what it can do, and whether you can stop it. An OSS answer to "Auth0 for AI Agents," built from Keycloak, OpenFGA, and OpenBao and reachable over MCP.

docker compose up Keycloak · OpenFGA · OpenBao non-production reference

Why this exists

One agent is convenient. Fifty is a governance problem.

Agents are multiplying — one per person becomes one per task becomes a fleet. Every one of them needs to act as someone. Hand each a copy of your credentials and, at scale, you can't answer the four questions that actually matter:

Q1
Who authorized this agent to act as me?
Delegation, not shared credentials — a login becomes a scoped, audience-bound token.
Q2
What exactly can it do — and nothing more?
Consent-gated, provider-scoped tokens plus fine-grained, per-user authorization.
Q3
Does a human decide when it matters?
Reactive CIBA step-up — a single-use, hash-bound approval before the sensitive act.
Q4
Can I stop it — right now?
Instant, per-grant revocation with a measured time-to-stop.

Prokura assembles the identity plumbing that makes all four answerable — proven end-to-end, running today. It's a reference architecture, not a SaaS: the blueprint you stand up with docker compose up, not a product you point at your org.
