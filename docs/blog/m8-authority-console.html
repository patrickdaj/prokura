Milestone 08 · Authority console

One place to read the register and tear up the grant

Power of attorney is only tolerable if you can see who acts for you and revoke it in one click. After M7 every party is correct, but the principal's own view was scattered across four surfaces and a log store. M8 is the "my agents" panel: one signed-in page showing every agent that acts for you, the grants each can use, your pending approvals, your notification topic, and a live activity feed — plus per-agent revoke and "connect a provider". It adds no new authority: it's a trusted surface that relays your own authority downstream by token exchange, with the broker still the sole writer.

Shipped · 2026-08-10 add-authority-console services/authority · :8160 correct-party gaps · all closed

M7 put the correct party in every capacity. But ask the actual question a delegator asks — "what acts for me right now, and how do I stop it?" — and v0 had no answer in one place. Agents and their consents lived in OpenFGA tuples; pending approvals in the approval service; the notification topic derivable only inside that service; the activity in Loki; grants in the broker. Five sources, four surfaces, one login each. M8 aggregates them behind a single OIDC session and adds the two verbs that make a register a governance tool: revoke and connect.

The headline

A trusted surface that relays your authority, never a service acting for nobody

The console never asserts who you are and never holds a service credential that acts on authorization state. It signs you in with its own confidential authority-ui client and keeps your tokens server-side (the cookie carries only signed claims). For every downstream read or action it performs an RFC 8693 token exchange — as the authority-console client — into the exact audience it needs: aud=token-broker to list and revoke consents, aud=approval to read your approvals and topic. The exchanged token preserves your subject, so downstream the owner is the verified token subject — the same guard M7 built, reused unchanged.

That is the whole trick to adding a surface without adding authority: the broker still writes every tuple, the approval service still owns the topic salt and every decision, and the console just carries your own bearer to them. A confused-deputy shape — a surface that asserts a username out of band — is exactly what this design refuses.

The register

Everything that acts for you, on one page

  • ✓My agents — every agent whose operator is you (read from OpenFGA directly, the console's own read position), each with its consented grants and scopes, and a Revoke button per grant.
  • ✓Pending & recent approvals — read from the approval service with your exchanged token; decisions still happen only on :8120, so each row deep-links there — the console links the ceremony, it never re-implements it.
  • ✓Connect a provider — routes you into Keycloak account linking (kc_action=idp_link) in your own browser; on return the console imports the grant with your exchanged token. Closes Flow B's last ❌ — no admin API, no demo driver.
  • ✓Notifications — your unguessable ntfy topic and a subscribe QR, from a user-bound read API. The topic salt never leaves the approval service; the topic stays notify-only (ADR-0007).
  • ✓Activity — the already-correlated Loki audit lines, filtered server-side to your username before anything leaves the backend, so one principal can never read another's activity.

One click, honest about what it does

Revoke converges on the broker; both paths, one audit event

Clicking Revoke exchanges your token for aud=token-broker and calls the broker's revoke — the same code path and same audit event as revoking from the M7 consent screen. Because revoke only ever deletes a tuple under grant:{you}/…, a foreign subject can never revoke your delegation. The console is honest about latency: effect applies on the agent's next token request — instant propagation is M9. Verified by looking: the tuple leaves OpenFGA, a consent_revoked … azp=authority-console line lands in Loki, and the revoked agent's next hand-out is refused 403 not_consented.

The separation invariant held, and grew. The authority service's source contains no user password and no CIBA or decision call — it is a surface, not a ceremony. test_party_separation.py greps it and fails the build if a password or a /decide call ever appears. (It may relay a revoke — that's the point — with your exchanged bearer, the broker still the sole writer.)
